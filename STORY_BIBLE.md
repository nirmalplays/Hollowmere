# STORY BIBLE: Hollowmere (working title)

Companion to `GAME_PLAN.md`. Dark fantasy. Grim, tragic, restrained. The player and up to 7 co-op friends are **the Unlaid**: champions who cannot stay dead. The threat is **the Hollow**, a spreading corruption. The twist that drives everything: **the Hollow is made of the death the Unlaid stole from the world.**

All names are original placeholders. Rename freely; keep the structure.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph pitch

Generations ago, Orsolen's Founder-King cheated death with a Crown, again and again, never knowing the price: every refused death lodged in the world like a stone in a stream. Behind those stones a mysterious plague began to rise, the Hollow. Nobody connected the two. A century ago, with the plague winning, the Lantern Wardens made a sensible guess with the facts they had: champions who could not die could not be taken by the Hollow. They performed the rite called **the Second Breath** and made eight of them. It failed in the worst way, because every death those champions refused would be another stone. The eight slept in the dark for a hundred years while the Hollow grew. You and your friends wake in a sealed cave beneath a ruined shrine, still alive, still unable to stay dead, in a world that has forgotten you. Slowly you learn the horrible truth: the Hollow is not an invader. It is the world's **unspent death**, backing up like water behind a dam, and you are part of the dam. Every time you fall and rise again, it grows. To save the world you must decide what to do about yourselves, and whether to repeat the mistake of every generation before you.

---

## 2. Tone and themes

- **Themes:** the cost of refusing death, grief that cannot be released, duty versus mercy, what we owe the dead, whether a life that cannot end still means anything.
- **Tone:** quiet dread, wounded tenderness, dry gallows humor from survivors. No grimdark nihilism: people still bake bread and plant gardens. The home base is the emotional counterweight.
- **Rule of three:** every major beat should land one of: *beauty*, *dread*, or *grief*. Avoid pure exposition scenes.
- **Show the Hollow, don't tell:** pale moss, black still water, grey-bone trees, birds that stop singing, villagers who forget a name one letter at a time. Visual decay in zones gives instant feedback.

## 3. World primer

### 3.0 Laws of Death (the rules the whole story obeys)

Every story idea, boss, quest and line of dialogue must be consistent with these. If something breaks a law, change the idea, not the law.

1. **Death is a passage, and it must flow.** Every life ends by passing through the world and onward. Think of it as a river.
2. **Refusing death blocks the flow.** A refused death is not erased. It lodges in the channel like a stone in a stream. The **Hollow** is the water backed up behind those stones.
3. **Blocked dead cannot pass.** People who die behind the blockage linger and thin into **Hollowed**: hungry, forgetful, still carrying echoes of who they were.
4. **Removing the cause does not clear the blockage.** Killing whoever refused death does not remove the stones they left. This is why the Hollow outlived the Founder-King.
5. **Only a bound life can touch the blockage.** A mortal who goes near the heart forgets themselves and Hollows. The Unlaid survive because they return, which is why only they can undo it, and also why they keep adding stones.
6. **A stone leaves only by being paid back.** Either a death is given willingly (a sacrifice), or the blockage is released slowly and safely through a ring of anchors (the Eight Lanterns), so the backed-up dead can finally pass.

**The history behind the laws:**
- **The Founder's crime.** Aldric the Founder, first king of Orsolen, used the Crown to die and return many times. Each return was a stone. He eventually stayed dead, but the stones stayed behind.
- **The plague with no cause.** Generations later the Hollow began to rise. The Lantern Wardens were founded to fight it and never learned why it existed. The Founder's secret was buried with his Crown.
- **The honest mistake.** The Wardens reasoned that champions who cannot die cannot be taken by the Hollow. With the facts they had, it was a sensible plan. It added eight more sources of stones.
- **The Queen's suspicion.** Queen Isaveth inherited a fragment of the Founder's diary and suspected the cost. She volunteered to be the **vessel**, hoping to absorb the damage herself. She became the heart of the blockage instead.
- **The theme.** Every generation tried to outwit death. The question the game asks is whether you repeat that mistake (Crown, Seal), pay it with your own life (Release), or finally do it the right way (Hearth's Return).

**How the laws map to gameplay:**
- **Hollow Pressure** = the height of the water. Each Unlaid death adds a stone (raises Pressure).
- A **Rest** (Lantern-lit finishing blow on a Hollowed) lets one trapped soul pass and lowers local Pressure a little.
- **Three phases of play:** *Contain* (keep Pressure low), *Learn* (ledgers, Memory Shards, ally truths), *Resolve* (the final choice at the Sunken Lantern).

### 3.1 Orsolen (the fallen kingdom)
A river-and-forest kingdom ruled from the city of **Highwell**. A hundred years ago it was the last human realm still standing against the blight. Its people were devout, practical, and exhausted. The kingdom's collapse is told in ruins, not speeches.

### 3.2 The Ashen Reach (v0.1 region)
The kingdom's old border province. Now a scatter of villages, hunting trails, and ruins. Named for the grey ash-like spore the Hollow sheds.

### 3.3 The Hollow
- **What people believe:** a plague/curse from the old gods, a creeping rot that unmakes living things.
- **What it is:** death with nowhere to go. Things that should have ended do not end; they *thin*. Creatures become **Hollowed**: still moving, hungry, wrong, remembering fragments of who they were.
- **How it spreads:** outward from places where an Unlaid has died. Each resurrection leaks a little unspent death. The first sign in any area is *forgetting*: names, faces, songs.
- **Hollowed creature rules (for design):** they carry echoes of their former life (a Hollowed blacksmith swings a hammer; a Hollowed child hums a lullaby). Enemy flavor text and idle barks should use this.

### 3.4 The Second Breath (the rite)
Devised by **Archwarden Corvane Ashmere** and approved by **Queen Isaveth the Last**. Eight volunteers, one for each of the Eight Lanterns, were bound so that when they die, their "breath" returns to them. The rite required a **vessel**: a sacrifice of something permanent. The Wardens took that to mean "the Queen's own mortality" and she agreed. What they did not know: refused deaths are never erased, they lodge in the world (see Laws of Death, 3.0). The Founder-King had done the same long before, and the Wardens had never learned why the Hollow existed.

### 3.5 The Unlaid (the player characters)
- Eight were bound. **Players are up to eight of them** (matches co-op cap). Other Unlaid, if there are fewer players, are the ones the story turns into **bosses and tragic NPCs** (see 7).
- Why they woke now: the seal over the Underroot Caves weakened when the Hollow reached the shrine above.
- Amnesia: not total. The Unlaid have **fragments**, recovered through gameplay as "Memory Shards" (see 9).
- **In-world slang:** townsfolk call them *the Unlaid*, *the Restless*, or in whispers *Tally* (because each of their deaths "adds to the tally"). Some villages believe killing an Unlaid permanently is a mercy. Some villages worship them. Most just fear them.

### 3.6 Factions
| Faction | Who they are | Stance on the Unlaid |
|---|---|---|
| **Lantern Wardens** | Order that performed the rite. Nearly extinct. | Guilty, secretive; one survivor holds the truth |
| **Thornwick Folk** | Villagers of the first hub | Wary, grateful, scared |
| **The Rootwives** | Hedge-witches of Greywood who tend the forest | Know the Hollow is *grief*; want to ease it, not fight it |
| **The Ashen Court** | Surviving nobles who feed on the Hollow's power | Want to **keep** the Hollow and control it (endings) |
| **The Pale Choir** | Cult of Hollowed-touched mortals who want the world to end gently | Believe the Unlaid should die for good, violently if needed |
| **The Hollowed** | The corrupted | Not a faction; the world's wound |

## 4. Death as a mechanic (story and gameplay tied together)

This is the core design hook. Implement via `GAME_PLAN.md` combat and save systems.

- **Dying is not failure, it is a cost.** On death the player rises at the last **Hearth** (home base or a lit shrine) after a short sequence.
- **Hollow Pressure** (world stat, host-owned): each Unlaid death raises the local zone's Pressure by a small amount. High Pressure makes zones darker, spawns more Hollowed, and changes ambient audio. Pressure slowly falls when the party clears Hollowed nests and completes cleansing tasks.
- **Scars** (character stat): each death has a chance to add a **Scar**: a small permanent-until-cleansed debuff (e.g. -3% max HP, a spell flickers, slightly reduced stamina regen). Scars are cleansed at the home base shrine for materials. Too many Scars unlock *Hollow-touched* options (powerful but risky traits) feeding the Ashen Court ending.
- **Forgetting:** each Scar costs the character a *Memory Shard* (a lore fragment is hidden until recovered again). Reinforces theme: the more you die, the more you forget.
- **Co-op:** a downed player is not instantly respawned: allies can **Rekindle** them (revive) within a window, preventing a death and its Pressure cost. Encourages cooperation.
- **Design guardrail:** dying must feel meaningful but never punishing enough to destroy a run. Scars are always cleansable.

## 5. Structure: acts and regions

v0.1 covers **Act I only** (the Ashen Reach). The bible below plans the whole arc so early content seeds later payoffs.

### Act I: *The Waking* (Ashen Reach) [v0.1]
Awaken, learn you cannot die, find the first human settlement, recruit allies, destroy the first great Hollowed threat.

### Act II: *The Ledger* (Marrowfen and the drowned Highwell approaches)
The truth about the rite begins to surface through Warden ledgers. Betrayals and factions harden. Pressure becomes a world-wide problem.

### Act III: *The Choir and the Court* (Highwell, the ruined capital)
The Pale Choir and the Ashen Court both act. The fallen Unlaid appear as bosses. The player learns what the vessel actually was.

### Act IV: *The Reckoning* (The Sunken Lantern / the origin site)
The final choice. Four endings plus one failure ending (Section 8).

Region list (for later zones; stub as data now): Ashen Reach, Marrowfen (flooded bogland), the Salt Terraces (dead sea cliffs), Highwell (capital ruins), the Sunken Lantern (origin).

## 6. Act I in detail (v0.1 target content)

### Zone 1: Underroot Caves (tutorial dungeon)
- **Opening:** You wake in a stone cradle in a sealed crypt. A cracked lantern burns. Other cradles are around you: some empty, some shattered, one still glowing. In co-op, each player wakes in their own cradle; cradles that nobody occupies are marked with a name carved into the stone (a hint at the other Unlaid).
- **Teaching beats:** movement, stamina/dodge against crawling Hollowed, block/parry, first death (a scripted unavoidable death teaches resurrection and the Scar system with no penalty).
- **Lore beat:** carved wall inscriptions in the old tongue (readable later after Brother Tamsin teaches you).
- **Mini-boss: The Gaunt Sexton**, a Hollowed gravedigger still tending graves that no longer exist. Telegraphs: shovel sweep, grave-pit AoE. Drops **Sexton's Key** and the first Memory Shard.
- **Exit:** a stairwell up into a shrine ruin overlooking a grey, quiet valley.

### Zone 2: Thornwick Village (hub)
A fortified hamlet of about 40 people. Wary of you. The quest board is here. Training yard is a half-collapsed barn. NPCs:
- **Maren Thorne**: herbalist-innkeeper, practical, sharp-tongued, grieving. Lost her son Wyn to the Hollow. Gives **alchemy** facility. Recruitable ally #1.
- **Brother Tamsin Rook**: the last surviving Lantern Warden, a thin scholar who has been hiding in a cellar. Knows more than he admits. Gives **scribing** (spellbooks). Recruitable ally #2.
- **Korrin Dunehorn**: exiled Bullhorn smith, blunt, loyal once earned. Gives **forge** facility. Recruitable ally #3.
- **Elder Halsey Pruitt**: village head, hostile to the Unlaid at first ("the Tally brings the Hollow"), softens or hardens depending on choices.
- **Pip**: a child who can *hear* the Hollow. Not recruitable in v0.1; appears as a recurring presence and later ally.

**Thornwick main quests:**
1. *The Quiet Valley*: investigate why the birds stopped. Teaches Hollow Pressure.
2. *Name on the Stone*: Maren asks you to find a lost relic from her son; leads into Greywood.
3. *What the Cellar Knows*: Tamsin trades the first piece of the truth for protection.

### Zone 3: Greywood (forest, open exploration)
- A fog-heavy forest where the Hollow has begun to "forget" the trees: some are bark-pale husks.
- **Rootwife Camp:** a neutral, mysterious group tending the edge of the Hollow with songs and salt. Source of the *Cleansing* mechanic and the first hint that **the Hollow is grief**.
- **Points of interest:** a goblin-analog hideout (rename: **Scrapfang hollow**), a hidden Warden outpost with ledgers, a half-drowned chapel.
- **Boss: Edrin Vale, the Unrested.** Warden-Captain and one of the *eight Unlaid*, the one who woke first, long ago, and has been fighting the Hollow alone for a century, slowly Hollowing from the inside. He recognizes you. Phase 1 he fights to *protect* you from himself; phase 2 the Hollow takes over. Defeating him gives a choice: **grant him rest** (permanent death, possible once because he is already Hollowed) or **bind him** (he returns as a hostile boss later). This choice is the **first major story flag.**
- **End of Act I:** Edrin's last words reveal there were *eight*, that the rite had a vessel, and that "the Queen did not die." Roll credits on Act I with a teaser.

## 8. Endings (heavy depth requirement)

Four main endings and one failure ending. All differ on **what happens to the world** and **what happens to you**. The choice is made at the base of the Sunken Lantern, where Queen Isaveth kneels in the pool of unspent centuries. You set the Eight Lanterns in a ring around her and place your hands on the rim of the central basin. Then the prompts appear:

- **[The Release]** Shatter the Vessel. Cast off your breath.
- **[The Seal]** Draw the Vessel inside. Lock the gate from within.
- **[The Crown]** Take the Vessel's weight. Reign over the stillness.
- **[The Hearth's Return]** Steady the Vessel. Call the names home. *(Appears only if its requirements are met.)*

| Ending | The world | You (the Unlaid) | Tone | Final-fight style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Release** | The pool is flushed out violently. The Hollow ends and the Hollowed vanish with no reunion. The land heals slowly, scarred | You die permanently. The world forgets the Unlaid existed | Noble, tragic | You hold the line while the Vessel breaks |
| **Seal** | Safe but frozen, no growth or change | Alive forever in the dark as the lock holding the pool shut, visited by ally descendants over centuries | Lonely, eternal | You drive the Queen back and bind her |
| **Crown** | Survives under dread, ruled by you | You become the new tyrants. Former allies turn on you and the epilogue is a rebellion | Villainous | You fight the Queen for her power |
| **Hearth's Return** (true ending) | Fully healed. Every Hollowed regains their name, spends one night with the living, and passes on in peace | You become mortal again and live out your remaining decades in the town you held together | Warm, earned, bittersweet | You hold the ring of Lanterns while allies work, surviving waves of the Queen's grief in a non-lethal fight |
| **The Wake** (failure) | The Hollow consumes the world | You are left as the only immortals, wandering an empty land among your allies' graves | Bleak | None; a quiet walk |

### Ending I: The Release
You shatter the Vessel and cast off your breath. The stored deaths flush out of the world in one violent surge. The Hollow ends, but the Hollowed are simply unmade, with no goodbyes and no reunions, and the shockwave scars the land around the origin site. The Unlaid die where they stand, permanently, and the surviving companions bury them. In the epilogue the world slowly recovers and the people forget the Unlaid ever existed.

### Ending II: The Seal
You draw the Vessel inside yourselves and lock the gate from within. The Hollow stops spreading and the world is saved, but nothing in it grows or changes. The Unlaid remain at the Sunken Lantern forever, immortal and alone. The epilogue skips across centuries: descendants of your allies come to visit, then fewer, then none.

### Ending III: The Crown
You take the Vessel's weight and reign over the stillness. The world is alive, but ruled by dread and kept alive by the stolen deaths, exactly as the Founder did. Your companions turn against you one by one. The epilogue shows a rebellion forming and the player characters as tyrants waiting for it.

### Ending IV: The Hearth's Return (owner-written; true ending)

**Requirements to unlock:**
- Edrin Vale granted permanent rest.
- All companion and hub personal quests completed (Maren, Tamsin, Korrin).
- Sorrel Anwick and Anselm Duren spared, allied, and present at the Sunken Lantern.
- All of the Queen's Memory Shards restored to the Vessel.
- Global Hollow Pressure below threshold (the Ledger balanced).

**The final choice.** At the base of the Sunken Lantern, Queen Isaveth kneels within the stagnant pool of unspent centuries. The air is thick with cold ash and silent weeping. Instead of drawing steel or striking the Vessel, you step forward with your surviving companions and set the Eight Lanterns in a ring around her. Tamsin breaks the Wardens' seals; Sorrel begins the Rootwives' cleansing hymn. The player characters place their hands upon the rim of the central basin, willingly channeling the locked power not into themselves, and not tearing it open to violently flush the earth, but steadying the flow through the Eight Lanterns.

**The sequence.** When chosen, the harsh white light of the lanterns softens into the amber glow of a hearth fire. The pressure wave does not hit like thunder. A warm, quiet wind rushes through the sunken crypt, up the roots of the world, and out across the valleys of Orsolen. Across the Ashen Reach, the fog thins. The black, stagnant pools begin to ripple and clear. The pale, chalky moss sloughs away from the bark of greywood trees, uncovering green sapwood underneath. The transformation among the Hollowed is immediate, quiet, and profound:

- In the ruined fields, a Hollowed farmer stops swinging a rusted sickle into the dirt. The grey pallor leaves his skin. He drops the iron, looks down at his hands, breathes in clean night air, and blinks. He remembers his own name.
- In the cemetery outside Thornwick, the Gaunt Sexton lays his spade down against an old stone slab, sits upon the moss, and closes his eyes, weeping tears of pure exhaustion and relief.
- In the village square, Maren Thorne drops her lantern as footsteps approach the barricade gate. It is Wyn. He is not a shambling horror humming half a tune, but her boy as he was, pale, whole, and fully awake. He steps into the light, looks his mother in the eyes, and whispers her name.

There is no panic. There is only an overwhelming stillness as a century of stolen clarity rushes back into every wandering soul.

**The parting.** They do not stay, because they were never meant to. Through the night, the restored dead walk back into the villages and hamlets. They do not demand food, nor do they bleed. They sit by the fires with their sons, daughters, and great-grandchildren. They speak of who they were. They tell the old stories lost to the collapse. They give directions to hidden granaries and buried springs, name the boundaries of fields, and speak the final words that were choked off a hundred years ago. As the dawn breaks over the mountains, the first true, gold dawn Orsolen has seen in a century, the natural order gently reclaims its debt. Wyn hugs Maren one last time, whispers that he is tired, and steps out onto the porch. As the morning light touches him, his body does not rot or burn; he simply dissolves into soft, drifting ash that scatters harmlessly on the wind. Across the valleys, the restored dead smile, lay their heads down, and drift away into quiet, dignified sleep. The debt is paid. Death has returned to being an ordinary, peaceful mercy.

**Epilogue: The Living Valley.**
- *The fate of the world:* The Ashen Reach is no longer ashen. The ground is rich with century-old leaf-rot, clean water runs down the streams from Highwell, and the birds are singing at full throat. With the knowledge bequeathed by their ancestors during the night of return, the villagers clear the brambles, re-plow the terraced hillsides, and expand the fortifications into true homesteads. Anyone who passes away in the seasons that follow dies quietly in their bed, surrounded by candles, passing on without fear of the grave.
- *Maren Thorne* tends her herb gardens with steady hands. She still wears Wyn's whistle around her neck, but she no longer listens for ghosts. Her remedies heal living people.
- *Brother Tamsin* burns the old, deceptive Warden ledgers and writes a new history, one carved into stone so that no king or council can ever claim that death can be outsmarted.
- *Korrin Dunehorn* melts down the weapons of the Vigil to forge plows, nails, and hearth-hooks for the new homes rising in the valley.
- *The fate of the Unlaid:* The curse of the Second Breath unwinds slowly, like thread from a loosened spool. You do not die where you stand. Your wounds do not kill you tomorrow. But when you cut your hand on stone, the blood runs red, and it leaves a scar that stays. You are mortal again. You live out your remaining decades in the town you held together. You help timber the barns, you teach the children how to hold an axe, and you sit beside the tavern fire watching the snow fall on quiet roofs. When your hair turns white and your breath grows thin with age, you lie down in a bed made by your neighbors, close your eyes, and are laid to rest beneath the green earth, finally, completely, and in peace.

**How it fits the Laws of Death (3.0):** the Lanterns release the blockage slowly (Law 6), so the backed-up dead can finally pass (Laws 2 and 3), and the Unlaid's own stones are paid back gradually instead of all at once.

### Ending V: The Wake (failure ending)
Triggers automatically if Hollow Pressure crosses a world-wide threshold before the final choice, or if a key ally dies at the wrong moment. There is no final fight. The Hollow consumes Orsolen and the Unlaid walk an empty land among the graves of their allies, unable to die. A short, quiet sequence, meant to give the other endings their weight.

### Unlock summary
- **Release and Seal:** available to nearly everyone who reaches the Sunken Lantern.
- **Crown:** requires Ashen Court alignment above a threshold and a minimum number of Hollow-touched traits.
- **Hearth's Return:** requires every condition listed in Ending IV.
- **Wake:** automatic failure state (see above).
- **Co-op:** the final choice is a **vote** of connected players, with the host breaking ties; the options offered are limited to the endings the world has unlocked.
- **Epilogue cards:** per-ally fates, per-region state and your home base tier, shown at the end and driven by flags.

## 9. Lore delivery systems (for deep-lore requirement)

- **Memory Shards (character-bound):** ~40 collectible fragments of the Unlaid's pasts. Each recovered shard unlocks a short flashback text/cutscene. Losing Scars can hide shards until recovered again.
- **Warden Ledgers (world-bound):** ~25 readable books/pages found in the world explaining the rite, the cost, and the Order's failures. Stored as world-collected flags.
- **Environmental storytelling:** gravestones with last words, abandoned tables set for dinner, forgotten names scratched out on walls.
- **Ally conversations:** relationship levels unlock lore the ally withheld.
- **Rootwife songs:** audio/text lore delivered as in-world songs (original lyrics only, written by you).
- **Codex:** in-game journal sorted into People, Places, The Rite, The Hollow, Creatures. Entries unlock on discovery.

Store in `WorldSave.flags` (host-owned) unless noted. Quest/dialogue lives in `data/quests/` and `data/dialogue/` per `GAME_PLAN.md`.

```

korrin_blade_quest: "open" | "reforged" | "failed"
ally_alive: {tamsin, maren, korrin, ...}
rootwives_standing: -100..100
ashen_court_standing: -100..100
pale_choir_standing: -100..100
ledgers_found: [ids]
boss_defeated: [ids]
ending_unlocked: {release, seal, crown, hearths_return}

# Character flags (CharacterProfile)
scars: [ids]
memory_shards: [ids]
hollow_touched_traits: [ids]
deaths_total: int
```

Rules:
- Dialogue lines can have `requires` (flag conditions) and `sets` (flag changes).
- Quests are state machines in data: `states`, `transitions`, `rewards`, `on_complete.sets`.
- Ending availability is **computed** from flags by one function (`story/ending_resolver.gd`) and unit-tested.
- **Co-op rule:** story choices that change world flags require the **host's** decision unless the quest marks `vote: true`, in which case connected players vote and the majority (host breaks ties) decides.
- Character-bound lore (Memory Shards, Scars) travels with the character to any world.

## 11. Ally personal quests (relationship system)

Relationship meter 0-5. Levels unlock dialogue, then a **personal quest**, then a **facility upgrade**. Early-game three:

**Maren Thorne** (Alchemy)
- Lv1: she opens the shop. Lv3: *Wyn's Whistle*: find the last thing her son made; the whistle carries a Hollow echo of his voice. Choice: **let the echo go** (she grieves, heals) or **keep it** (she stays haunted but gets a buff-potion recipe). Affects `maren_son_quest` and the Hearth's Return ending.
- Lv5: Alchemy tier 2, can brew Cleansing Draughts that remove Scars.

**Brother Tamsin Rook** (Scribing)
- Lv1: spellbooks. Lv3: *The Cellar Ledger*: reveals he **knew** the rite's cost and stayed silent. You can forgive, shame, or expel him; each path changes the truth he shares. Lv5: a ritual that lets you read the old-tongue inscriptions and recover lost Memory Shards.

**Korrin Dunehorn** (Forge)
- Lv1: forge. Lv3: *The Blade of the Vigil*: reforge Edrin's broken blade. Outcome changes depending on `edrin_fate`. Lv5: legendary weapon upgrade tier.

Later allies (stub files): **Pip** (hears the Hollow; key to the Hearth's Return), **Sorrel Anwick**, **Anselm Duren**, a Hollowed who retains his mind (**Ash**), a smuggler with Court ties.
